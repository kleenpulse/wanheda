## State in one paragraph

The Phase 5 exit is measured and holds. A cold open of a 300-page PDF paints page 1 in 420 ms (1.5 s with the CPU slowed 4×). Scrolling 91 pages has no long task. Both books turn by every key and by swipe, and under reduced motion turns are instant. Proving "close on the desktop, open on the phone, same page" exposed a real bug (B20): a device opening from its stale library snapshot stamped the old place with a fresh clock, overwriting the newer place from the other device. It is fixed: the open keeps its place's own clock, only the owner's moves are saved, and an open reader moves to a newer place from elsewhere. Testing the book's keys exposed a second bug, on `main` since T49 (B21): Markdown and text books could only turn forward, because of a bug inside page-flip. It is patched around. Nothing is committed yet.

## Decisions made

- Only owner moves are saved (Builder call, in V41). The alternative, comparing locators, breaks on ‰ rounding.
- Following a newer place moves the open view without asking (newer `at` wins, V41). A Kindle-style "go to page N?" prompt would be a UI decision for the user.
- `openedAt` is optional on the progress write. Older clients and the beacon keep working.

## Bugs found

- B20 (V41): stale snapshot + fresh clock on open and restore ⇒ another device's place overwritten. Fixed.
- B21 (V42): page-flip `flipPrev` without the left offset + `disableFlipByClick` ⇒ text books forward only. On `main` since T49. Fixed by `fixBackTurn`.

## Known problems and risks

- `fixBackTurn` reaches into page-flip internals (`flipController.flip`, `.render.getRect`), like `guardCornerHover` and `neuter` already do. page-flip is pinned at 2.0.7; re-check these if it is ever bumped.
- The 4× CPU scroll cost per page (pdf.js main-thread paint) is the next perf lever if a phone feels it.
- `useResume`'s gate opens once per open, not per move: after the first owner input, a later restore (resize, zoom) saves under a new clock. Reviewer nit, taken: the gate closes again whenever the tab comes back into view, so a tab left open while another device reads needs a fresh move before it saves. Closed on return, not on hide, so a report still in flight as the tab hides is kept. Reviewer FYI, not changed: following a newer place while auto-scroll runs closes the gate, so auto-scroll saves again only after the next input.
